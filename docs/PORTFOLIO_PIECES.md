## 01. Blobby's Woodland Wonder

**Project period:** 2020–2026  
**Created:** 2020  
**My role:** Founder / product engineering · ListPull

A mobile game that connects animal-matching puzzles, stack sorting, village building, and exploration in one woodland world.

Blobby's Woodland Wonder is one of the mobile products I work on through ListPull. The challenge is bringing several puzzle mechanics into one game without making it feel like a collection of unrelated screens. Animal matching, stack sorting, village building, and exploration all behave differently, but the player still needs to understand how they fit together. That takes consistent menus, clear feedback, and a lot of attention to what happens when someone taps, moves, or finishes a level. My role as founder covers product direction and engineering, alongside the wider creative work that goes into the game. I include it because mobile development calls for a different kind of thinking from a business dashboard or cloud service. There is still application state to manage and features to connect, but timing, movement, and the feel of the experience matter just as much.

## 02. Creative Editing Applications

**Project period:** Active in 2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** Founder / creative application engineering · ListPull

Image and vector editing work focused on layered artwork, object transforms, reusable design controls, and reliable import and export workflows.

Creative software has been part of my work since I automated artwork production in Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop. At ListPull, that experience carries into applications for working with images and vector graphics. The problem is not just putting a drawing surface on the screen. Someone might import artwork, move an object, change its layer, adjust a shape, undo a mistake, and export the result. Each step needs to agree about what changed. The engineering work is in representing the artwork, keeping selections and properties in sync, and making sure edits do not disappear when someone switches tools. It also means designing controls that are precise without slowing people down. This project brings together the creative work I started with and the application development I've done since. It's an example of solving a production problem with software, not just making a screen that looks like an editor.

## 03. Video Editing & Production

**Project period:** Active in 2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** Founder / media application engineering · ListPull

Video production software that brings footage, layered timelines, titles, audio, previews, and export tasks into a connected editing workflow.

Editing video is a good example of why an application's behavior matters as much as how it looks. A production workflow has to keep footage, clips, timing, titles, audio, and exports organized. A change to one clip affects what appears at a particular point in the timeline, sometimes alongside several other tracks. The engineering challenge is keeping those pieces in sync while someone makes changes and checks the result. At ListPull, media production also connects with our wildlife publishing and creative projects, so the editing work is tied to the practical needs of making and preparing content. This project shows my work around interactive controls, media handling, previews, and the details that make a complicated workspace manageable. There is a lot going on behind a simple trim or title adjustment. The goal is to keep that complexity out of the person's way.

**Featured:** 2026

## 05. Market Analytics & Data Platforms

**Project period:** 2025–2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** Founder / full-stack and data engineering · ListPull

A market analytics platform bringing multiple data feeds together through APIs, backend services, authenticated views, and time-series charts.

Market data gets complicated when it comes from different providers. Each feed can have its own format, update schedule, missing values, and limits. I worked on a ListPull analytics platform that brings those sources into a common research interface. The engineering involved React and Angular views, PHP services, WordPress-backed authentication, external API integrations, and time-series charts. The hard part wasn't drawing a graph. It was collecting, organizing, and serving the information so the graph had something useful behind it. That also meant thinking through what happens when a provider is slow or its data changes. My work connected the front end to the services and data feeding it. I include this project because it shows the full-stack side of analytics, from getting information into the system to making it readable on screen. The outcome is a research view that stays usable even though the information comes from several separate systems.

## 06. Forecasting Services

**Project period:** 2025–2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** Founder / forecasting service engineering · ListPull

Node.js forecasting services using ARIMA models, per-symbol caching, data-provider integrations, and intraday and daily projections.

Forecasting is useful when it is part of a working data pipeline, not just a line added to a chart. I built forecasting services for ListPull using Node.js and ARIMA models, with per-symbol caching and intraday and daily projections. That work sits on top of data retrieval and time-series handling. The service needs to keep observations tied to the right symbol, avoid repeating expensive model work when it can reuse a result, and return estimates without treating them like measured facts. The interface has its own job: show where the real observations stop and where the projection starts. I include this work because it connects backend services, statistical models, provider integrations, and clear data presentation. It also involves practical limits, like incomplete data and rate limits from outside services. That makes forecasting a service and integration problem as much as a modeling problem.

- Related browser example: https://listpull.com/studio/forecast.html

## 08. Clinic Recommendations

**Project period:** 2023–2025  
**Created:** 2023  
**My role:** Software engineering manager · Accenture

An AI-assisted clinic recommendation feature built into a React application, backed by AWS workflows, PostgreSQL, Bedrock, and OpenSearch.

At Accenture, I led engineering work on a clinic recommendation feature for Bristol Myers Squibb. The feature was added to an existing React application and used lab information to help surface relevant clinic options. On the back end, the work involved AWS Step Functions, Lambda, PostgreSQL, Bedrock embeddings, and OpenSearch. My role included helping the team connect these pieces and make the result usable in the application people already had. What I took away from this project is that AI features don't live on their own. They still need dependable data, clear rules, careful testing, and an interface that makes sense to the person using it. This was also sensitive work, so the portfolio doesn't show private data or a real client screen. The diagram is a simplified view of how the systems fit together, not a copy of the production setup.

**Created:** 2019

At BAE Systems, I helped modernize the public Bureau of Labor Statistics website for the U.S. Department of Labor.
