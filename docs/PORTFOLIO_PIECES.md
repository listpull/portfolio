## 01. Blobby's Woodland Wonder

**Project period:** 2020–2026  
**Created:** 2020  
**My role:** ListPull founder

Blobby's Woodland Wonder is a puzzle game published by ListPull, the company I founded. Players can match animals, sort stacks, build little houses, and move through a colorful woodland world. I like this project because it brings together two sides of my work that don't always show up in the same place: writing software and making something people enjoy spending time with. A game has to feel simple when you're playing it, even when there's a lot happening behind the screen. The menus, game rules, artwork, and way each level feels all have to work together. As ListPull's founder, I help guide the product and its engineering. I'm not claiming I personally created every character or visual. What I'm proud of is helping bring the whole experience together and making it available on phones.

## 02. Creative Studio

**Project period:** Active in 2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** ListPull product

Creative Studio is a browser-based editor for working with pictures and simple designs. You can bring in an image, add text or shapes, arrange things in layers, and export the result. I wanted this tool in my portfolio because it shows the kind of software I enjoy building: something practical that people can open and use without learning a complicated setup first. The challenge is making those details feel straightforward without losing useful options. This is part of the ListPull Studio collection, and the link takes you to the working editor. ## 03. Video Studio

**Project period:** Active in 2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** ListPull product

Video Studio gives people a place to put together clips in their browser. The idea is familiar: bring in video, work on a timeline, make cuts, add titles, adjust sound, and export what you've made. ## 05. Business Insights

**Project period:** Active in 2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** ListPull product

Business Insights is a way to explore a set of numbers without having to build a dashboard from scratch. You can choose a sample about inventory, customers, deliveries, or wildlife, or bring your own CSV file. ## 06. Forecast Lab

**Project period:** Active in 2026  
**Featured:** 2026  
**My role:** ListPull product

Forecast Lab lets you take a set of past numbers and see what a model thinks might happen next. You can start with sample data or upload a CSV, look at the trend, run a forecast, and compare the model with earlier results. I've also worked on forecasting services using Node.js and ARIMA, so this is an area I know from the engineering side as well. The most important part for me is keeping the difference between real data and an estimate easy to see. A forecast can be useful without being certain, and the screen shouldn't pretend otherwise. Charts need clear labels, sensible time ranges, and room for a result that isn't perfect. This is a tool for exploring patterns, not a promise about the future. The illustration uses example values, and the link opens the app.

- Try Forecast Lab: https://listpull.com/studio/forecast.html

## 08. Clinic Recommendations

**Project period:** 2023–2025  
**Created:** 2023  
**My role:** Software engineering manager · Accenture

At Accenture, I led engineering work on a clinic recommendation feature for Bristol Myers Squibb. The feature was added to an existing React application and used lab information to help surface relevant clinic options. On the back end, the work involved AWS Step Functions, Lambda, PostgreSQL, Bedrock embeddings, and OpenSearch. My role included helping the team connect these pieces and make the result usable in the application people already had. What I took away from this project is that AI features don't live on their own. They still need dependable data, clear rules, careful testing, and an interface that makes sense to the person using it. This was also sensitive work, so the portfolio doesn't show private data or a real client screen. The diagram is a simplified view of how the systems fit together, not a copy of the production setup.



## 09. Public Health Systems

**Project period:** 2021–2023  
**Created:** 2021  
**My role:** Application architect and senior engineer · Accenture

At Accenture, I worked on public health systems that had to pass information between different tools and teams. One part of the work connected Salesforce contact center operations with AWS Connect, so calls and follow-up steps could move through a more organized process. I also worked on digital vaccination record access and disease tracking workflows. These projects taught me how much time can be lost when people have to repeat a step or copy information from one system to another. The technical work included APIs, cloud services, routing rules, and data handling, but the reason for building them was practical: make the work easier for the people doing it. I worked as a senior engineer and later in an architecture leadership role. The image is a general diagram, not a government system or an actual patient record.



## 10. Bureau of Labor Statistics Website

**Project period:** 2019–2021  
**Created:** 2019  
**My role:** Lead web developer · BAE Systems

At BAE Systems, I helped modernize the public Bureau of Labor Statistics website for the U.S. Department of Labor. I worked on reusable React components, charts built with Highcharts, and pages that pulled in government data through APIs. I also wrote tests and helped with publishing tools for articles, images, and PDFs. This kind of work isn't flashy, but it matters. People visit public data websites because they need to find a number, understand a chart, or check something they heard. If the page is hard to use or the data is presented poorly, the information isn't doing its job. I learned a lot about accessibility, careful testing, and keeping a large website consistent while different teams work on it. The image here is an illustration, not an official BLS screenshot. You can follow the link to the public site.

- Visit BLS.gov: https://www.bls.gov/
